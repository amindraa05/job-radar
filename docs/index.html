How the score is built, and what it cannot see

Role is a gate, not a bonus. A marketing posting that mentions AWS is still a marketing posting. Titles are classified first, and anything that is not an infrastructure role scores zero regardless of its content. A block list removes the near misses: physical security systems, pre-sales solution architects, QA and mobile roles that share vocabulary with infrastructure.

Seniority is penalised honestly. The profile is worth about two and a half years. Senior, staff, principal and management titles carry large negative weights rather than being hidden, because occasionally one is worth a speculative application.

Geography is the sharpest filter. Many roles advertised as remote are restricted to one country. Those are detected and pushed to the bottom with the restriction named. Roles requiring relocation are kept but marked, since they need a visa.

What it cannot see: salary in most cases, whether the posting is still open, whether the company sponsors visas, and anything on LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, Jobstreet, Glints or Kalibrr. None of those publish an API, and scraping them would breach their terms. Indonesian coverage is therefore thin and comes mostly from global companies hiring in Jakarta or Singapore. Treat this as one input, not the whole market.

Scoring is a heuristic, not a judgement. Every row shows the reasons behind its number so you can disagree with it. Green tags are skills the CV supports; amber tags with a question mark are technologies in the posting that the CV does not currently claim.

Built as an engineering exercise: scheduled ingestion from 87 public job boards and four remote-work APIs, de-duplication, rule-based scoring, and a static dashboard refreshed by GitHub Actions. Source and method are in the repository. Postings belong to their respective employers; this page links to them and stores nothing about you.
